# Phase 4B: Lazy SMP and measured multicore scaling

## Decision

Retain configurable parallel search. On this M5 Pro, four workers achieved
**3.25× aggregate throughput** in the one-second screen and finished nominal
depth 6 **2.09× faster** than one worker. All nine measured four-worker timed
comparisons completed one additional depth. These are bounded performance
observations, not a playing-strength result. Threads remains 1 by default;
classic timing, LMR off, and MVV-LVA remain unchanged.

## Implementation

`Threads` accepts 1–64. One hash table and immutable network are shared; board,
history, killers, PV, and NNUE accumulators are private to each worker. Cache
reads copy complete entries under striped mutexes in multiworker mode; the
single-worker path takes no mutex. Workers vary equally ranked root moves.
The leader owns the reported score, PV, completed depth, and soft-clock choices.

All workers share cancellation, ponder origin, hard deadline, and one exact
node budget. Helpers are joined on completion and exception paths before any
state can be reconfigured or freed. A root/history capture race found during
review was corrected before the recorded test and benchmark executions.
An independent second review found no further shared-state defect.

Completed-depth UCI reports include aggregate published nodes; the final
depth-free `info nodes ... time ... nps ... hashfull ...` includes unfinished
work and exact joined totals. Opt-in `smp v1` identifies individual worker totals.
Iteration diagnostics remain leader-only; other event totals aggregate workers.
The implementation and diagnostic contracts are in [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Fixed screen

The [plan](../PHASE_4B.md) declared three previously exposed `tools/bench.py`
positions, Threads 1/2/4, Hash 64, pinned NNUE, classic timing, LMR off, MVV-LVA,
ponder/diagnostics off, and 20 ms move overhead. Measurements used:

- One 1,000 ms search and one depth-6 search per position/configuration.
- One warmup plus three measured repetitions, rotating/reversed worker order.
- 72 total searches: 18 warmups and 54 measured searches; nine measured pairs
  per mode and nonbaseline worker count.
- One resident engine, with cleared table/learned state and completed readiness
  before every search. External monotonic go-to-bestmove latency includes
  worker startup, search, output, and helper shutdown.
- Independent `chess==1.11.2` replay of every PV, bestmove, and ponder move;
  exact final counters, input hashes, and per-sample resource checks.

The run completed from 21:51:04 to 21:52:10 Helsinki on October 18: about 66
seconds, within the 120-second cap. Host: macOS ARM64, M5 Pro, 18 logical CPUs,
64 GiB RAM, Apple Clang 21.0.0, generic release build with NEON. Four active
workers fit the daytime allowance of nine. Conservative reserved memory was
1,065,133,372 bytes; this is a planning estimate, not measured peak RSS or an OS
utilization cap. No compiler or other agent-run engine search ran alongside.

## Results

Ratios below are geometric means of nine matched root/repetition ratios.
Throughput counts all workers, including redundant work. Depth latency compares
the same nominal requested depth, not identical search trees or node counts.

| Workers | Timed aggregate throughput / 1 worker | Depth-6 latency speed ratio | Timed completed-depth change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 1.00× | 1.00× | Baseline |
| 2 | 1.83× | 1.58× | +1 in 5/9; unchanged in 4/9 |
| 4 | 3.25× | 2.09× | +1 in 9/9 |

Four-worker timed throughput ratios ranged 2.89–3.93×; depth-6 latency ratios
ranged 1.61–2.57×. One-second requests ended around 980 ms because the configured
20 ms overhead is reserved. Four-worker median throughput across the three roots
was approximately 730k, 644k, and 669k nodes/second, versus 240k, 202k, and 183k
with one worker. Timed depths changed 6→7, 5→6, and 5→6. All measured best moves
matched; depth-6 scores also matched in all measured comparisons. Timed scores
changed with the deeper completed searches.

The separate parent/candidate one-worker comparison passed all 24 searches
(six warmups and nine measured pairs): every completed depth, selective depth,
node count, score, PV, bestmove, and ponder move matched. Root median paired
parent/candidate latency ratios were 0.9814, 0.9527, and 0.9916; their geometric
mean was 0.9751. This suggests a small single-worker cost (roughly 2.6% longer
latency by that summary), with observed variability and only three measured
repetitions. Do not claim a single-worker speedup or statistical significance.
No further tuning was performed against these screen results.

## Validation

Passed on native macOS ARM64:

- Full `make -j2 test`, scalar `make -j2 SIMD=scalar test test-smp`, and
  ASan/UBSan `make -j2 MODE=sanitize test test-smp`.
- Native `make test-smp`: 43 bounded independent-referee UCI/lifecycle cases.
  The same 43 cases passed in scalar, ASan/UBSan, and ThreadSanitizer builds.
- `make -j2 MODE=thread-sanitize test-search test-smp`: direct search tests
  and UCI suite completed with no reported races or sanitizer findings.
- 24 scaling-profiler tests, covering malformed/illegal results, null moves,
  final counter accounting, incomplete pairing, resource admission, stale
  post-search output, pin changes, and failure cleanup.

Direct search checks include two/four workers, global tiny node caps, shared
Hash geometry, legal PVs, immediate cancellation, policy changes, repetition,
mate/rule-50 boundaries, time/pondering, root restrictions, and recovery after a
throwing reporter. Concurrency tests are evidence for exercised schedules, not
a mathematical proof. Only two/four-worker native ARM64 execution was qualified;
the advertised 64-worker limit and foreign platforms were not exhaustively run.
NNUE implementation is unchanged; the full external SF19 stage oracle was not
rerun. No new Cute Chess visual check or SMP strength match is claimed.
