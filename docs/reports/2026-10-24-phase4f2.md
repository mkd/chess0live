# Phase 4F.2 — move-ordering efficiency

Date: October 24, 2026. Parent: `928d821eac535b63f0d968d477138e453314f317`.
Decision: accept cached move scoring and reusable worker-local storage, with
unchanged search priorities and policy defaults. This is an implementation
optimization; no Elo, pruning-default promotion or release is claimed.

## Change and invariants

The former default ordering comparator recalculated both move scores for every
sort comparison. SEE ordering already computed scores once, but allocated its
temporary vector at every ordering call. Both modes now share one decorated
sort: compute each score once, retain the existing optional SEE adjustment,
sort by the same descending score/tie comparator, and copy every move back.
Each search worker owns its reusable temporary vector; capacity survives nodes.

All scores are refreshed at every call. The former comparator's position,
history, killers and reply inputs were immutable during the sort, so caching
them within that call preserves order. The complete list is copied before
recursion; no reference to temporary storage escapes. A child's ordering cannot
rewrite its parent's list. There is no fixed maximum move count or per-ply
scratch allocation. Learned history is neither cleared nor cached across calls.

TT, promotion, capture, killer, reply and history precedence remains unchanged,
as do helper-worker root ties and the ordinary lexical tie fallback. SEE still
examines every eligible ordinary non-TT capture, even in a singleton list, with
the original counter deltas. No move is inserted or pruned by this change.
ProbCut sees the same complete ordered pre-pass; LMR receives the same move
numbers. Staged/lazy selection was deliberately left for a separate change
because rescoring after child searches could observe updated history.

## Bounded current-default comparison

The unchanged `tools/profile_search_baseline.py` ran all 15 exposed development
roots at depth 6, twice, alternating AB/BA. Each role used a cold search followed
immediately by a warm search. Settings were Threads 1, Hash 64 MiB, pinned NNUE,
LMR on, MVV-LVA, classic timing, other experiments/pondering/tablebases off.
Readiness was outside measurement. One engine searched at a time; two were
resident, under the 50% daytime budget on an 18-logical-CPU/64-GiB ARM64 host.

All **120 searches / 60 pairs** matched exactly. Independent audit reconstructed
720 iteration records and replayed 3,104 PV moves plus every final best/ponder
move. Nodes, scores, depths, selective depths, PVs, best/ponder and hash occupancy
matched. Repetitions were deterministic; the full pair order and all 35 recorded
source, binary, tool, network and corpus identities passed before/after checks.

| Cache state | Pairs | Nodes per build | Parent seconds | Candidate seconds | Speed ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fresh | 30 | 3,701,668 | 12.0108 | 11.3852 | 1.05495 |
| Reused | 30 | 790,712 | 2.29835 | 2.21729 | 1.03656 |

Ratios divide parent elapsed time by candidate elapsed time: approximately
**5.5% higher fresh-cache throughput and 3.7% higher reused-cache throughput**,
or 5.21% and 3.53% less elapsed time. The per-pair geometric mean ratios were
1.0467 and 1.0370. Twenty-eight fresh pairs and 25 reused pairs improved.
The warm `opening-02` case was slower in **both** repetitions: combined
288.357 ms versus 308.938 ms, approximately **7.14% more time**. Warm `eg-02`
was essentially unchanged in aggregate. These regressions are retained, not
discarded or retuned away. The aggregate result supports this small shared
implementation, not a promise that every position is faster.

The run completed in 28.650 seconds within its 240-second limit, on macOS
26.7.1 ARM64. Samples ranged from 1.13 ms to 1.37 s; only two repetitions were
run. The maximum wall/monotonic discrepancy was 9.75 microseconds, with no
observed suspension. The initial process snapshot showed the owner's two
console engines idle; it is not continuous load or thermal telemetry. Warm
state includes TT and learned history. No foreign-host speed result, confidence
interval, playing match or universal performance claim follows.

  ordering check. The full ASan/UBSan `make test` also passed with that check.
  Forced-scalar Search API and ordering checks passed in a separate build.
- `test-ordering-search` passed **2,264 comparisons** against a frozen test-local

  singleton lists, TT/killers/reply/history overlap, signed history values,
  worker IDs 0/1/3/9 at root and interior plies, both colors, en passant,
  castling, all promotions, stale flags and positive/zero/negative SEE.
  Position and learned state stay unchanged. Changed histories are rescored;
  smaller sorts reuse capacity without modifying previously returned move lists.
- Native null-move, ProbCut, quiescence-SEE and deep-quiet search tests passed,
  including their activation, state/cancellation and worker checks. The
  countermove UCI suite passed 57 cases; SMP passed 43 lifecycle/search cases.
- Each of the owner's unchanged **39 FENs** was searched at **20,000 nodes**
  using both parent and candidate, first with current defaults and then with
  all eight search settings enabled. All 156 searches passed exact parity
  within their policy package: 260 default and 268 all-enabled iteration
  records, plus final nodes/moves/ponder, with independently legal PVs.
  The total was 3,120,000 nodes. No run was replaced or retried. This is not a
  timed mate-solving audit, evidence of complete selective-branch activation,
  or a comparison of strength between the two configurations.

No sanitizer findings or search-equivalence failures were observed. There is
no new shared state or synchronization. No long puzzle/match campaign, foreign
runtime execution or TSan rerun was performed for this checkpoint.
